## Sources for the claims on screen

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| A $100 note costs about 9¢ to produce | Federal Reserve, *Currency Budget* — FY2023 unit cost of the $100 note: 9.4¢ |
| "Double coincidence of wants" | Standard term in monetary economics for the barter problem money solves (Jevons, 1875) |
| Shells, salt, cattle and gold used as commodity money | Long-standing economic history; cowrie shells across Africa/Asia, salt in the Sahara trade, cattle as a unit of account, gold and silver coinage |
| Until 1971 dollars were convertible into gold | Under Bretton Woods, foreign governments — not the general public — could convert dollars to gold at $35/oz. U.S. citizens' gold convertibility ended in 1933. |
| Nixon suspended it in August 1971 | Announced 15 August 1971; known as the "Nixon shock" / closing the gold window |
| "Fiat" = "let it be done" | Latin *fiat*, third-person singular present subjunctive of *fieri* |
| ~97% of money is bank deposits | Bank of England, *Money creation in the modern economy*, Quarterly Bulletin 2014 Q1 (UK money supply) |
| Banks create money when they lend | Same Bank of England paper: "the act of lending creates deposits" |
| Germany, 1923: a loaf of bread from ~250 marks to ~200 billion marks | Weimar hyperinflation, January → November 1923 |
| Zimbabwe, 2008: prices doubled about every day | At the November 2008 peak, daily inflation was roughly 98%, a doubling time of about 24 hours |

## A note on the title

"Money isn't real" is a hook, and the video is careful to land the accurate version of the
claim: money has no intrinsic physical value and is not a natural object, but it is a real
social institution with real, enforceable consequences — closer to the rules of a game or
the words of a language than to a rock. The closing line ("an agreement we renew every day")
is the point, not the provocation.
