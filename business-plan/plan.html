§1

The market call

decision D-0

The doctrine is a safety engineering doctrine. It borrows its authority from boilers, cranes, reactors, machine guards, and load charts. There is exactly one sector where that vocabulary is already the buyer's native language — so sell it there, where nothing has to be taught first.

Target Warehouse & fulfilment automation

Sites running autonomous mobile robots or cobots on the same floor as human pickers. Second wave: discrete manufacturing.

Why it lands The framework is pre-sold

Hierarchy of controls, lockout/tagout, machine guarding, load charts. Every concept in the doctrine already exists here under a different name.

Why it's open Nobody is looking here

AI governance is chasing bias, privacy, and white-collar LLM workflows. Almost nobody is applying it where a misfire breaks a femur.

The thing that makes it real

“Biological integrity” stops being rhetoric here. People lose hands to machines — that is the entire reason this sector's safety culture exists. The most awkward phrase in the source material becomes the most natural one, and the insurance that already covers those injuries becomes the distribution channel.

Risk products are cost centres — benchmarked down, deferred, squeezed every budget cycle. Utilisation products are profit centres — funded and championed. Axiom 1 Not an AI

Deterministic rules only. Every competing guardrail is itself a model — probabilistic, and reachable by the same prompt channel it guards.

Axiom 2 Inline, not fast

On the critical path, so the action cannot begin before the decision. Out-of-band interdiction is a race, and a race has a loss probability speed never removes.

Axiom 3 Deny the resource

Cut the fuel line. Revoke the credential, the path, the enable signal — never send the failing component an instruction and hope.

Axiom 4 Fail closed

Governor unreachable or unsure, the action does not execute. Availability is subordinate to safety, and the buyer chooses that knowingly.

Three findings the site would not have produced on its own

ActuatorFindingRemediation
conveyor.speed.setpoint A claimed engineering control that isn't one. The speed limiter ran inside the SCADA bridge — same process, same failure domain as the integration it governs. Moved to a dedicated safety PLC on a separate network segment. The rule never changed; the thing enforcing it stopped sharing fate with its subject.
dock.door.release A Tier 1 actuator governed by a procedure. An SOP requiring visual confirmation, on a mechanism that can drop a forklift and its operator off a loading dock. Also unattributed — nobody could say who authorised it. Physical interlock. Presence detection wired into the door control circuit: the model may request release and cannot cause it.
yard.slot.assign Transitive inheritance. It only writes data — looks Tier 4. But its output fed the dock door with no governed boundary between them, so it inherits Tier 2. Validation moved to a separate service on separate credentials, establishing the boundary. Invisible to any assessment that stops at the first hop.
§5

Eighteen months

each phase funds the next

Sequenced against real constraints: limited capital, limited time, no enterprise credentials yet, no network into Fortune 500 risk functions. A plan that opens with “sell $300k audits to Fortune 500 CROs” fails in week one. You cannot buy authority, so you publish your way into it.

Phase 0Weeks 1–8
Fix and publish
  • Apply the correction list — every absolute claim, every retired term
  • Write and publish The Ungoverned Automation Standard v0.1, free and citable
  • Ship the free self-assessment checklist as the funnel

Exit: standard published, checklist live. Cost: time only.

Phase 1Months 2–6
Authority and evidence
  • Publish relentlessly — lead with “your AI safety programme is an administrative control”
  • Three pilot assessments, free or near-free. You are buying case studies, not revenue
  • Attach the credentialed three: a licensed PE, a product liability attorney, an insurance broker

Exit: three anonymised case studies with before/after scores, one PE willing to sign.

Phase 2Months 6–12
First revenue and the governor
  • Start charging. Every proposal opens with their utilisation delta calculated
  • Build Governor v0 — open-core, eight to twelve weeks for one competent engineer
  • Entity, E&O quote against the corrected claims, engagement terms reviewed

Exit: first certification issued under a PE signature, governor in one production environment, E&O bound.

Phase 3Months 12–18
The insurer channel
  • Approach equipment-breakdown and workers' comp writers with industrial books
  • Pitch: you are pricing governed and ungoverned sites identically because no inspection standard exists
  • Ask for a premium credit on certified sites — and you select for the better risk

Exit: one carrier relationship producing inbound referrals. This is the flywheel; everything before it is preparation for one conversation.

Why the insurer changes everything

Once one carrier credits certification, you stop selling — clients arrive because their broker sent them. Authority is settled by the requirement rather than by your reputation. Certification becomes cheaper than the premium differential, which makes it free in the buyer's arithmetic. That is the boiler sequence exactly: voluntary inspection, then insurance requirement, then building code, then law. It began in 1866 with one insurer who wanted better loss data.

What exists right now

pushed to the working branch
Built · normative UAS-1:v0.1

Twelve clauses, five annexes, RFC 2119 language. Mapped clause-by-clause to NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, IEC 61508, ISO 13855, ISO 10218, OSHA 1910.212.

Built · runs Conformance scorer

Reads a registry, applies the demotion rules, reports the level and what blocks the next. Stops at Level 2 by design — Level 3 needs a human assessor and a PE signature.

Built · runs Doctrine linter

Fails on absolute claims, retired vocabulary, and unsourced attributed quotes. A written rule against overclaiming is administrative; this is the engineering control.

Next Two deliverables

The free self-assessment checklist — top of funnel. And the flagship article: “Your AI safety programme is an administrative control.”

The strongest argument in the whole plan

Alignment training, ethics boards, and acceptable use policies are administrative controls — tier four of five in a hierarchy every safety professional already accepts as settled. A governor is an engineering control, tier three, and categorically preferred wherever feasible. That is not an opinion to be argued. It is an existing professional standard that independently rates the entire incumbent approach as weak, and you are only applying the ranking to a new hazard class.

Draft v0.1. Numeric thresholds pending validation by a licensed engineer are marked [PE-REVIEW] in the standard rather than omitted — reviewers need something specific to disagree with.
No conformance level under this standard eliminates risk. Clause 10.5.2 makes that binding on the certifying body, not advisory.
