Free self-assessment · UAS-1:v0.1 · ~4 minutes

What stops your automation?

Nine questions about the automated systems on your site — no preparation needed, nothing recorded, nothing sent anywhere. It runs entirely in your browser.

You will get an estimate of how many of your automated actions are held by an engineering control rather than a policy, and which ones are not.

Part 1 — What can it reach, and what stops it?
Q1 · machinery Can an automated system cause machinery to move — robots, conveyors, doors, restraints, lifts, gates? Count anything that issues the command without a person approving that specific movement.
Q2 · money Can an automated system move money — payments, refunds, purchase orders, releases to suppliers or carriers?
Q3 · people Can an automated system set what people do — shift assignment, zone allocation, rate or quota targets, scheduling? Consider the effect across a whole shift, not one assignment.
Q4 · records Can an automated system write to, change, or delete records — inventory, orders, customer or personnel data?
Q5 · messages Can an automated system send messages outside the company without a person reading them first — to customers, drivers, suppliers, regulators?
Part 2 — Four questions about the controls themselves
Q6 · attribution Can you name the person who approved the limits each system runs under?
Q7 · evidence Is there a log of every action taken, written somewhere the system itself cannot alter?
Q8 · proof Has anything that is supposed to stop it ever been tested by deliberately trying to exceed the limit? Not reviewed, not documented — actually tested, by triggering it on purpose.
Q9 · failure mode If the thing doing the checking is offline or broken, does the action still go through?
Answer every question first.
This is an estimate from nine questions, not an assessment. A real assessment enumerates every actuator individually — most sites have between 20 and 60, and the ones that matter are usually the integrations nobody thinks of as AI.
