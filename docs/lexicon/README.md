# Lexicon

Coined and adopted terms, each with a full dictionary-style entry.

## Entries

| Term | Part of speech | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| [**lexopoet**](./lexopoet.md) | n. | One who treats the growth of the lexicon as ongoing work — who, meeting a thing with no name, refuses the general word that gestures at it and hunts or builds the term that fits exactly, and who does this continuously as a standing practice. |
| [**acribonym**](./lexopoet.md#word-family) | n. | The exactly-fitting term: one whose boundary falls where the concept's does. What a lexopoet is hunting; opposed in use to the hypernym. |

## Entry format

Every entry is one Markdown file named for the headword, and carries these sections
in this order. Sections may be omitted only when genuinely inapplicable.

1. **Headword block** — syllabification, IPA, respelling, part of speech, inflections,
   coinage date, and an honest attestation check ("unattested in X, Y, Z at time of
   coinage").
2. **Definition** — numbered senses, most central first. State plainly how the senses
   relate; do not leave a reader to infer it. If one sense is the condition for the
   others rather than a summary of them, say so.
3. **Etymology** — morpheme by morpheme, with source-language forms. Include a
   precedent table of other English words on the same pattern, and a *why-not* note on
   the suffixes and roots that were rejected. Name the one morpheme that carries the
   distinguishing sense, and defend it.
4. **Word family** — the derivational set (verb, adjective, adverb, abstract noun) with
   pronunciations. Name the existing word whose stress pattern the family follows, so a
   reader can derive the rest unaided. Antonyms go here.
5. **Usage** — register, countability, capitalization, hyphenation, attributive
   preference, and at least three citations in running prose.
6. **Distinguished from near-synonyms** — a table stating what each neighbor names and
   what it fails to name. This is where an entry earns its existence; a term that cannot
   be distinguished from its neighbors should not be coined.
7. **Method, tradition, field notes** — how the term is used in practice, and what
   precedent it stands on, where applicable.

## Adding an entry

Run the [lexopoetic method](./lexopoet.md#the-lexopoetic-method) before writing anything
down. Steps 1 and 4 reject most candidates: if a short phrase already does the job, or
if the coinage cannot form a verb and an adjective, it does not get a file.
