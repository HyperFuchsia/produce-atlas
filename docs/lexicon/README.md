# Lexicon

Coined and adopted terms, each with a full dictionary-style entry.

## Entries

| Term | Part of speech | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| [**lexiurge**](./lexiurge.md) | n. | One who hunts out words they lack in order to gain the thoughts those words carry, and who mints new terms where the hunt comes up empty. |

## Entry format

Every entry is one Markdown file named for the headword, and carries these sections
in this order. Sections may be omitted only when genuinely inapplicable.

1. **Headword block** — syllabification, IPA, respelling, part of speech, inflections,
   coinage date, and an honest attestation check ("unattested in X, Y, Z at time of
   coinage").
2. **Definition** — numbered senses, most central first. State plainly how the senses
   relate; do not leave a reader to infer it.
3. **Etymology** — morpheme-by-morpheme, with the source-language forms. Include a
   precedent table showing other English words built on the same pattern, and a
   *why-not* note on the suffixes and roots that were rejected.
4. **Word family** — the derivational set (verb, adjective, adverb, abstract noun),
   with pronunciations. Note the existing word whose stress pattern the family
   follows. Antonyms go here.
5. **Usage** — register, countability, capitalization, hyphenation, attributive
   preference, and at least three citations in running prose.
6. **Distinguished from near-synonyms** — a table stating what each neighbor names and
   what it fails to name. This section is where an entry earns its existence; a term
   that cannot be distinguished from its neighbors should not be coined.
7. **Method / field notes** — how the term is used in practice, where applicable.

## Adding an entry

Run the lexiurgic method in [`lexiurge.md`](./lexiurge.md#the-lexiurgic-method) before
writing anything down. Steps 1 and 4 reject most candidates: if a short phrase already
does the job, or if the coinage cannot form a verb and an adjective, it does not get a
file.
