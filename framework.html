Six termsThe framework at a glance

The entire framework can be compressed into six working terms. Each one links to the page where it is fully defined, defended, and bounded.

Dignity — standing
The person's inherent human worth. It is not issued by employment, health, strength, wealth, or public approval, and it is never revoked by performance. Dignity & sovereignty →
Integrity — condition and coherence
The dynamic condition and coherence of the whole person across embodied health, cognition, emotion, identity, relationships, conduct, agency, and participation. It is not a score of worth. The integrity architecture →
Sovereignty — bounded authority
Meaningful, informed, voluntary self-government over one's own body and life — never authority over another person. Dignity & sovereignty →
Responsibility — duty
What a person owes, measured against actual capacity, power, knowledge, and realistic alternatives. Moral proportionality →
Accountability — evidence-based response
The post-failure process: what happened, what harm occurred, what power and capacity existed, what consequence is proportionate, what protection is required, what prevents recurrence. Moral proportionality →
Environment — context and shared causation
The surrounding conditions a person lives inside — what families, workplaces, institutions, and markets reward, punish, normalize, and extract. Environment & reciprocity →

How it arguesThe critical method

The framework's strongest contribution is not a single slogan or health feature. It is the refusal to choose between two morally inadequate positions: treating male existence as presumptively dangerous, defective, or socially suspect; or protecting male people by placing male conduct beyond scrutiny. The whole of Not a problem to solve develops this refusal.

Critical method

The project is strongest when it refuses two opposite errors at once: denying harm in the name of personal responsibility, and denying personal responsibility in the name of harm.

Do not use identity to excuse conduct. Do not use conduct to indict the identity.

Every page that follows applies this method to one domain — the body, the mind, relationships, institutions, power, disability, and the surrounding environment. Dignity protects the person. Accountability addresses the behavior. Neither principle is permitted to erase the other.

The argumentSix linked propositions

The framework is most coherent when stated in six linked propositions. Together they form one argument: each proposition limits the others, and none of them stands alone.

Proposition 1

Dignity is inherent

A male person's human standing is not issued by employment, health, strength, wealth, sexual success, provision, protection, endurance, sacrifice, relationship status, or public approval.

Proposition 2

Integrity is multidimensional and dynamic

It concerns the condition and coherence of the whole person across embodied health, cognition, emotion, identity, relationships, conduct, agency, and participation. It is not a score of worth or a demand for perfection.

Proposition 3

Sovereignty is bounded self-government

It means meaningful, informed, and voluntary participation in decisions about one's own body and life. It never means authority over another person, exemption from fair law, or immunity from consequences. Dignity & sovereignty →

Proposition 4

Accountability is conduct- and evidence-based

It asks what happened, what harm occurred, what power and capacity existed, what duties and alternatives were present, what consequence is proportionate, what protection is required, and what can prevent recurrence. Moral proportionality →

Proposition 5

Health and environment affect agency without mechanically determining morality

Human judgment is embodied. Sleep, pain, illness, injury, medication, stress, and neurological functioning may affect particular capacities, but illness does not create immorality, health does not guarantee character, disability does not reduce dignity, and diagnosis does not prove incapacity.

Proposition 6

Responsibility is reciprocal

Society cannot demand healthy, responsible, emotionally regulated, trustworthy, ethically self-governing men while normalizing male disposability, chronic humiliation, sleep deprivation, contradictory expectations, health neglect, responsibility without authority, and sacrifice without reciprocity. Environment & reciprocity →

HonestyWhere the framework stands as evidence

"Male dignity, integrity, and sovereignty" is an original integrated ethical framework built by transparent synthesis across human dignity, public health, autonomy support, disability rights, supported decision-making, workplace health, trauma, stress, social connection, coercion, development, ethics, and accountability. It is not one validated diagnosis, clinical scale, legal test, scientific law, or settled academic theory — and it does not claim to be.

Limit

The synthesis is not automatically a standardized scientific construct merely because it is named. Every substantive empirical claim on this site carries its evidence status — established evidence, reasonable inference, philosophical synthesis, emerging research, or claim requiring further investigation — on the Evidence page, together with the public correction record.
