Deep Survey — the commercial lighter Magpie

You are eleven light years out with a standing order and nobody who understands you.

SEVENTY-ONE real stars, at their real right ascension and declination, out to the edge of a licence that stops covering you at twenty-five light years. Plot the jump yourself or let the computer do it badly. Fly down into a system on the stick and put the ship alongside a world you have never seen.

Four cultures are already out here. None of them speak to you and none of them will start. You learn a script the way anyone learns a script — by hearing enough of it — and the console shows you exactly how much of a sentence you did not get.

Others are less patient. They read your drive signature, they appear on the sweep at the rim of the scope, and they close every time you move. They speak plainly. That is the worst part.

  • Real astrometry. 71 catalogued systems, nothing invented.
  • Plotted jumps. Three numbers, and the ship goes exactly where they point.
  • First-person conn. Fly to the planet on a rate stick and dock alongside it.
  • Four alien tongues, each with its own voice, learned a glyph at a time.
  • Hostile traffic that follows you, takes what you have found, and can be bought.
  • Cultural contests — tally, hold, precedence — because diplomacy is a game you can lose.
  • Air, hull and fuel spend themselves. One of them spends itself faster when you are holed.
