Claim governanceThe standard this page enforces

Whole Man must distinguish strong evidence from plausible interpretation, publish limitations, expose unresolved questions, preserve version history, and correct errors publicly. Coined terms may be useful, but naming a pattern does not prove the pattern scientifically or legally — every project term carries an explicit status in the glossary.

Every publication must distinguish among established evidence, plausible interpretation, project terminology, ethical argument, and open question. The site must never present an internally coined term as a medical diagnosis, a legal offense, or a universally accepted academic construct. Editorial corrections must be visible and dated. The site must publish its methods, sources, conflicts of interest, professional review, and known limitations.

Certainty is never used as a design aesthetic when the evidence is uncertain.

This page also serves as the framework’s permanent criticism page: the strongest good-faith objections to the project are stated here fairly and kept public, alongside the corrections they have already produced.

Framework statusOriginal synthesis, not settled science

“Male dignity, integrity, and sovereignty” is an original integrated ethical and product framework. It is not one validated diagnosis, clinical scale, legal test, scientific law, or settled academic theory. Its strength comes from transparent synthesis across human dignity, public health, autonomy support, self-determination, disability rights, supported decision-making, workplace health, trauma, stress, social connection, coercion, development, ethics, and accountability. The full architecture of that synthesis is described in the framework overview.

Public evidence categories

Every substantive statement on this site belongs to one of five public evidence categories: established evidence; reasonable inference; philosophical synthesis; emerging research; or claims requiring further investigation. The next step of the project is to operationalize these labels at the level of the individual claim — the claim-card standard below.

MethodHow claims are graded

Two published instruments govern grading. The evidence ladder states how each kind of evidence must be represented — including the framework’s own synthesis, which sits explicitly below empirical tiers. The publication tiers state what a label on a published claim is allowed to mean.

Publication tiers — what a claim label is allowed to mean
Evidence tierPublication meaning
ConfirmedSupported by credible evidence, direct documentation, or well-established professional consensus.
Supported but qualifiedEvidence exists, but effect size, causation, generalizability, or individual application remains limited.

The July 2026 audit confirms the preferred order of evidence: systematic reviews and rigorous meta-analyses; professional consensus and authoritative public-health guidance; longitudinal and well-designed experimental research; official datasets with denominators and demographic stratification; replicated observational research; carefully labeled qualitative and lived-experience evidence; and, last, single studies with prominent limitations. Alongside that order, the project must actively avoid the standard failure patterns of health publishing:

  • correlation presented as causation;
  • group averages applied to individuals;
  • cherry-picked sex comparisons;
  • neuroscience imagery treated as moral proof;
  • undated statistics;
  • one source stretched across multiple claims;
  • AI-generated citations that have not been manually verified.

Disclosure standardThe claim-card standard

Every substantive empirical claim on this site should eventually carry a claim card disclosing:

  1. the exact claim;
  2. the claim type;
  3. the population and setting studied;
  4. evidence strength;
  5. primary sources;
  6. relevant counterevidence;
  7. limitations;
  8. association versus causation;
  9. date last reviewed;
  10. reviewer and conflicts of interest;
  11. jurisdictional or demographic limits;
  12. and what evidence would change the conclusion.

Three worked examples follow, built from claims this site actually makes. They show the format the project is committed to rolling out claim by claim.

Verified baseCurrent evidence anchors

The project’s present public-health direction rests on several strong official anchors, each verified in the July 22, 2026 audit:

  • The World Health Organization defines brain health across cognitive, sensory, social-emotional, behavioral, and motor functioning. [E9]
  • The WHO identifies excessive workloads, low job control, job insecurity, discrimination, and inequality as mental-health risks at work. [E10]
  • The CDC reports that the 2024 U.S. suicide rate among males was nearly four times the female rate, with 48,824 suicide deaths recorded overall. These figures justify serious male-focused prevention while requiring demographic and causal nuance. [E11]
  • The WHO describes suicide as multifactorial, noting associations with financial problems, relationship disputes, chronic pain, illness, and moments of crisis. [E12]
  • CRPD Article 12 and its associated interpretation support legal capacity and supported decision-making rather than blanket removal of agency — the foundation of the disability and capacity pages. [E13]
Limit

These sources support public-health seriousness. They do not by themselves prove every philosophical proposition in the framework.

TestabilityFalsifiability and completion criteria

Operational claims must include observable completion criteria. A harmful mechanism is not dismantled because it was condemned; access, incentives, secrecy, authority, or recurrence must actually change. A trust-restoration program is not successful because participants report feeling inspired; evidence must show clearer expectations, reduced harm, justified trust, or restored autonomy. A wellness tool is not beneficial because users spend more time on it; it must improve understanding, access, decision quality, or appropriate care navigation. The glossary defines this as the testable completion criterion.

The platform never claims that reading content reduces suicide, prevents violence, restores brain health, or improves relationships without a valid evaluation design.

Subscriber retention is not wellness. Engagement is not dignity. Visibility is not impact.

Permanent criticismCritical questions the framework must keep open

These questions are published permanently, with the strongest good-faith objections stated fairly. Criticism is not treated as proof of hostility. Serious objections must produce visible revision when they expose a logical, ethical, factual, or operational weakness.

  1. Does individualized masculinity become too broad to define meaningfully?
  2. Could sovereignty language encourage antisocial individualism or dismissal of legitimate authority?
  3. Could failure-code language shield conduct from warranted criticism?
  4. Could anti-pathologization be misused to reject valid diagnosis or treatment?
  5. Could biological-integrity language create a hierarchy favoring healthy or nondisabled males?
  6. Could environmental analysis become a mechanism for avoiding personal accountability?
  7. Could male advocacy drift into grievance politics, misogyny, or competitive victimhood?
  8. Could “life-sustaining masculinity” become another compulsory masculine ideal?
  9. Could social-authority analysis become so broad that it delegitimizes ordinary feedback, institutions, and relationship standards?
  10. Could the civilizational title sound grandiose or imply exclusive male control?
  11. Could Whole Man itself become the identity-administering authority it criticizes?
  12. What evidence would cause a framework term to be revised, narrowed, or retired?

Because terms such as civilization, sovereignty, and masculinity carry ideological meanings, the framework’s moral language is itself capturable — risk R-31 in the consolidated register. The standing commitments against that capture are published definitions, plural perspectives, transparent sources, a dissent protocol, and no party alignment by implication.

The framework’s credibility depends on refusing emotionally satisfying claims that exceed evidence or create hidden contradictions. The following corrections are not concessions away from the vision; they are necessary protections against turning the project into the kind of coercive ideology it opposes. Under the version control rule, the corrected formulation governs wherever an earlier phrase was more absolute.

July 22, 2026 auditThe July 2026 red-team audit

Whole Man operates in a domain where language about sex, health, morality, accountability, power, autonomy, and civilization can be distorted from multiple directions. A benevolent intent is not a control. A sentence must survive reasonable skeptical reading, hostile quotation outside context, application to a person in crisis, application to a disabled person, application after serious harm, and use by someone seeking either domination or exemption.

The correct response is not to remove conviction. It is to make every conviction more exact about scope, evidence, affected people, limiting principles, and what it does not authorize.

Principal public-content contradictions

The audit identified five places where the live site’s earlier wording exceeded what the implementation or evidence supported. Each is published here in full, with the required correction — and each correction is now applied across this site.

1. “Collect no answers and save nothing”

The homepage described the private reflection tools as collecting no answers and saving nothing. That is accurate for the four no-answer prompts, but it could appear to describe the adjacent well-being check-in, which accepts selections and may store them locally when the visitor chooses storage. Required correction, repeated wherever both tool types are presented together:

The four private reflection prompts collect and save nothing. The optional adult well-being check-in processes your selections in this browser and stores them only if you choose a storage option.

2. “Anonymous check-in”

“Anonymous” is broader than the implementation evidence supports. The tool does not require a Whole Man account and is designed to keep answers in the browser, but the page still loads through ordinary internet infrastructure that may process network metadata. Browser extensions, managed devices, backups, malware, or device sharing also fall outside the site’s control. Preferred wording:

No-account, browser-only check-in. Whole Man does not receive your answers in the current version.

This says exactly what is verified without promising infrastructure-wide anonymity. The full analysis is on the privacy page.

3. “Professional provider directory”

Some copy suggested a professional provider directory. The current resource experience links to reputable external official directories; Whole Man does not presently maintain, vet, rank, match, or guarantee a proprietary provider network. Preferred wording:

Links to independent official care directories. Whole Man does not currently operate or endorse an individual-provider directory.

The corrected resource architecture is on the care page.

4. Governance tests and “blocked by tests”

The governance page stated that silent executive changes are blocked by tests. That is a desirable standard, but the current version-12 source and complete test suite were not independently recovered in the audit. Public wording must distinguish architectural policy from verified control: if automated enforcement tests exist in current source, identify them, run them, publish the last-test date, and retain artifacts; if the guarantee is primarily procedural, say “prohibited by policy and release procedure” rather than implying a proven technical block. The current wording is on the governance page.

5. “Fully functional”

The public editorial and browser-only functions are meaningfully functional. The product as a whole is not complete, because billing, accounts, professional care, community, provider operations, cross-device storage, automated governance, and qualified review systems remain closed. Preferred release language:

The public editorial beta and its on-device reflection tools are live. Paid, community, clinical, and automated-review services remain closed.

Precision here is a strength, not an admission of failure. Current operational status is detailed on About & current state and Membership & services.

Version 2 material corrections — framework era

Version 2.0 of the framework was not a second, disconnected manifesto. It was a critical reconstruction with four objectives: protect the central vision that the male person is a whole human being whose dignity, health, identity, relationships, agency, and sovereignty cannot be reduced to performance; make the claims defensible by distinguishing what history documents, what biology supports, what civilizational analysis reasonably infers, and what remains project philosophy; prevent the framework from becoming moral perfectionism, anti-social individualism, grievance politics, biological hierarchy, or immunity from accountability; and convert the philosophy into institutional standards that can govern a real website, publication, community, membership, and professional-care network.

Version control rule

Where a prior phrase is more absolute than the revised formulation, the revised formulation governs. No historical, biological, psychological, or civilizational claim should be published without its evidence status and limitations. The later corrections control wherever two versions appear to conflict.

Boundaries of the evidenceResearch caveats

The caveats from both research registers, merged and preserved in full:

  • Population-level associations cannot diagnose an individual. Health effects are probabilistic: associations and plausible mechanisms do not establish that a particular symptom was caused by a specific interaction without individualized evaluation.
  • Male-specific conclusions require male-specific evidence; general human evidence must be labeled when applied to males.
  • Historical examples demonstrate variation and institutional construction, not a single universal history.
  • Allostatic-load research is heterogeneous and must not be used to claim a specific biochemical injury from every emotional harm.
  • The masculinity and help-seeking literature concerns particular masculinity measures, cultures, and service environments; it is heterogeneous and culturally variable and often measures specific norms rather than masculinity itself. Masculinity itself is not a disease: the platform critiques rigid or coercive norms without treating masculinity as pathology.
  • Suicide statistics show disparity and urgency, not a single causal explanation.
  • Accessibility conformance does not eliminate the need for user testing and individual accommodation.
  • The terms “failure coding,” “masculinity capture,” “pathologizing mislocation,” “relational self-abandonment,” and “human-value extraction” are project terms, not formal diagnoses or universally standardized legal categories.
  • Legal duties involving accessibility, privacy, minors, telehealth, refunds, reporting, and professional practice vary by jurisdiction and change over time; legal and regulatory information must be rechecked before launch in each jurisdiction.
  • The platform must commission periodic independent review rather than treating any version as permanently complete.
