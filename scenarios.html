Apply · The framework in practice

The Scenario Library

Abstract principles need concrete application. These ten worked scenarios show how the framework analyzes real situations — without a default villain, without an identity verdict, and without pretending every conflict has a painless answer.

Every page in the framework states a standard. This page shows what the standards do. Each scenario below is examined through the same twelve dimensions — from known facts through prevention — so that the method itself, not the drama of any one story, is what you take away. Open any scenario in any order; each stands alone.

The methodHow each scenario is analyzed

Each walkthrough applies the same twelve analysis dimensions, in the same order. This is deliberate: the framework's claim is that a repeatable method — not intuition, loyalty, or outrage — is what makes moral analysis trustworthy.

Known facts
What is actually established — observable conduct, documents, timelines — separated from interpretation.
Uncertainty
What is disputed, unknown, or unknowable, stated honestly rather than filled in by preference.
Affected people
Every person or group materially affected, beginning with anyone harmed or at risk.
Power
Who held information, authority, money, physical capacity, institutional role, or leverage in the situation — and who did not.
Capacity
Whether health, sleep, pain, medication, disability, distress, or developmental stage affected specific relevant functions — assessed for the decision and moment, never presumed from a diagnosis or a state of distress. Capacity is not character.
Alternatives
What each actor could realistically have done instead, measured against the choices actually available — not the choices imagined from hindsight.
Individual conduct
What each person actually did, named accurately, with each person's responsibilities kept separate.
Environmental conditions
What was rewarded, punished, normalized, delayed, withheld, or neglected around the conduct — because repeated outcomes make the environment part of the evidence. Environmental analysis is never an acquittal.
Protection
What protects affected people now, before blame is allocated.
Consequence
What response is proportionate to the actual conduct — following the moral proportionality standard and the corrected foreseeability–preventability–accountability chain.
Repair
What repair is possible, and for whom it must remain voluntary.
Prevention
What must change — and be verified to have changed — so the outcome does not repeat.

Where duties genuinely conflict — safety against autonomy, truth against privacy — the walkthroughs use the framework's ethical conflict-resolution method: identify everyone affected, separate rights from preferences, weigh severity, urgency, probability and reversibility, choose the least harmful and least restrictive viable action, distribute burdens fairly, record the reasoning, and permit challenge and correction. The result may remain imperfect. Ethical integrity requires transparent reasoning, not the pretense of a painless answer.

DisciplineGround rules for this library

Core standard

Scenarios must avoid making women — or any group — the default antagonist. Across this library the actor, the mechanism, and the institutional role vary deliberately: sometimes the man is harmed, sometimes he causes harm, sometimes no one has done anything wrong and the question is how to protect everyone involved. Criticism identifies evidence, conduct, power, incentives, and mechanisms — never collective guilt. This is the same no-scapegoating, no-immunity rule that governs the whole site.

Three further disciplines apply. First, these are illustrative teaching cases composed to demonstrate the method — they are not reports of real, identifiable events, and they are not legal, clinical, or professional advice about any actual situation. Second, each walkthrough keeps context, symptom, and conduct separate: a man may be distressed without harming anyone, may harm someone while distressed, or may report harm accurately while communicating imperfectly. Third, no scenario ends in an identity verdict. The framework corrects conduct; it does not revoke manhood — and it does not excuse conduct because manhood was hard.

Ten casesThe ten scenarios

1 · Delayed safety feedback: responsible for a machine, without the information or the authority

A maintenance technician at a freight depot is disciplined after a conveyor guard he serviced fails and injures a coworker. The inspection checklist he was trained on had been revised four months earlier — the revision never reached his shift. Two earlier near-miss reports about the same guard sat unactioned in a supervisor's queue, and the technician had no authority to order replacement parts on his own.

Known facts
The guard failed; a coworker was injured; the technician followed the checklist he was actually given; the revised checklist was never distributed to his shift; two near-miss reports were filed and not acted on; parts authority sat above his role.
Uncertainty
Whether the revised checklist would have caught this specific failure; whether a reasonable peer working from the old checklist would have caught it anyway; the injured coworker's full prognosis.
Affected people
The injured coworker first; the technician facing discipline; every worker still using the line; the supervisor; the company.
Power
The employer controlled the information flow, the parts authority, the staffing, and the disciplinary process. The technician controlled only the work in front of him. Responsibility was assigned downward; the authority and information needed to discharge it were withheld.
Capacity
Nothing suggests impaired capacity. This is an information-and-authority problem, not a health problem — the dimensions must not be blurred.
Alternatives
The technician's realistic alternatives were narrow: he had already escalated through the near-miss system, which is what escalation is for. The employer's alternatives were wide: distribute the revision, work the near-miss queue, delegate parts authority to the people responsible for the parts.
Individual conduct
Judge his work against the standard he was actually given, not the one withheld from him. If any carelessness existed within his own checklist, that portion is genuinely his — environmental failure does not erase it.
Environmental conditions
Ask the environmental questions: what information or feedback was delayed or withheld? What authority was assigned with responsibility — and what authority was denied? Two ignored near-miss reports made this harm reasonably foreseeable at the institutional level.
Protection
The injured coworker's care and the line's immediate safety come before any allocation of blame.
Consequence
Apply the corrected chain: accountability attaches where knowledge, authority, capacity, control, and duty actually sat. The failure to distribute the revision and to act on the near-miss reports sits with the people who held that knowledge and authority. Discipline aimed solely at the technician mislocates responsibility — the pattern the framework calls extracting risk while withholding decision authority.
Repair
Support and compensation for the injured worker; correction of the technician's record where discipline was misassigned; honest acknowledgment to the shift.
Prevention
A working feedback pipeline; authority realigned with responsibility; verification — not assertion — that the near-miss queue is now actually worked.

Principles applied: foreseeability chain · reciprocity duties of employers · proportionality.

2 · Medical consent in severe pain: engaging refusal without presuming incapacity

A 58-year-old man arrives at an emergency department with a fractured hip, in severe pain, exhausted, with an opioid dose on board. At 2 a.m. he refuses to sign the consent form for recommended surgery. A resident notes "questionable capacity." A relative suggests getting the form signed "while he's groggy."

Known facts
Severe pain, sleep loss, and medication are all present; a refusal has been expressed; the decision is significant but not immediately life-threatening within the next hours.
Uncertainty
Whether pain, medication, and exhaustion are affecting this decision right now; what his settled preference would be under better conditions.
Affected people
The man foremost — it is his body; his family; the care team, who carry duty in both directions.
Power
The hospital holds the pain relief, the information, the timing, and the documentation. He holds the legal and moral authority over his own body.
Capacity
Decisional capacity is specific to a decision and a moment. Pain, medication, and 2 a.m. exhaustion can strain particular functions — comprehension, weighing, communication — without establishing general incapacity. Distress is not a diagnosis, and a diagnosis is not a verdict of incompetence. [R6]
Alternatives
The first response to a strained decision is support, not override: treat the pain first; re-approach after rest; explain more simply; invite a supporter he chooses; defer the non-urgent decision to the morning. A formal, decision-specific capacity assessment belongs only where refusal persists and the stakes rise.
Individual conduct
His refusal is not misconduct. It is the exercise of sovereignty that the system is obliged to engage honestly rather than engineer around.
Environmental conditions
Emergency departments produce close to the worst possible decision conditions — noise, interruption, fear, time pressure. The environment, not the man, is the first thing to fix.
Protection
Protect him from both harms at once: a surgery obtained through a groggy signature he never truly gave, and an unconsidered refusal driven by untreated pain.
Consequence
If a restriction on his decision authority ever became necessary, it would have to meet the framework's standard for legitimate limits: specific evidence, necessity, proportionality, the least restrictive workable measure, review, appeal, and restoration — never a blanket transfer of authority, and never the shortcut the relative proposed.
Repair
If the team pressured a signature, the repair is to say so, void it, and re-consent properly when conditions allow.
Prevention
Consent protocols that schedule significant decisions after analgesia and rest, and make supported decision-making the default rather than the exception.

Principles applied: supported sovereignty; capacity is not character · operational morality · legitimate limits on sovereignty.

3 · After harmful conduct: a survivor's right to no contact

After two years of escalating control — read messages, unannounced appearances, threats to share private photos — a man's partner ends the relationship, blocks every channel, and asks mutual friends not to relay messages. He tells the friends he has changed, that he deserves the chance to apologize in person, and that her refusal to hear him denies his dignity.

Known facts
A sustained pattern of monitoring, intrusion, and threat; the relationship has ended; a no-contact boundary stands; he is now seeking access through intermediaries.
Uncertainty
Whether his change is real. That uncertainty cannot be resolved by testing it at her expense.
Affected people
The survivor first; the man; the mutual friends being recruited as a channel around her stated boundary.
Power
He is attempting to convert past intimacy into present access. She holds the authority over her own contact, attention, and forgiveness — and that authority is complete.
Capacity
His distress after the loss is real and deserves care — from a therapist, a doctor, his own supports. It does not diminish his answerability, and it does not obligate her.
Alternatives
He can pursue change where it actually happens: treatment, accountability without an audience, written amends only if she ever asks for them, and leaving the friends out of it entirely.
Individual conduct
The monitoring and the threats were his conduct and require accurate naming. Pressing for contact through intermediaries is not repair — it continues the pattern under a moral vocabulary. The accurate finding is "you punished a boundary," not "you are not a man"; correction locates failure in conduct, not in malehood.
Environmental conditions
A peer culture that reframes persistence as romantic devotion supplies the mechanism. Naming it is part of disabling it.
Protection
Her no-contact boundary is a protective measure, fully compatible with his dignity. Respect is not access. Recognition of a person's worth never entitles him to another person's attention.
Consequence
Loss of the relationship, of trust, and of access are consequences — the recognition that former conditions no longer exist — not retaliation. Nothing she has done inflicts pain for its own sake.
Repair
Repair is voluntary for the person harmed and may be impossible, incomplete, or unwanted. No harmed person owes contact, forgiveness, education, emotional labor, or participation in repair. Here, repair means his verified changed conduct — not restored access.
Prevention
He internalizes the distinctions the framework insists on: recognition respect is unconditional; trust, access, admiration, forgiveness, and reconciliation are not. "Protect the male person" never means protecting access to a person he harmed.

Principles applied: survivor and affected-person safeguard; consequences are not retaliation · respect, trust, access, and forgiveness distinguished.

4 · Help-seeking under humiliation: when care is coded as failed manhood

A 24-year-old warehouse worker has stopped sleeping properly, quit his league team, and jokes at work that he is "not built for this." His friend group treats therapy as being for people who can't handle life; his father once called medication "giving up." A cousin has noticed the change and does not know whether saying something will make it worse.

Known facts
Functional decline across settings — sleep, activity, engagement; withdrawal from connection; self-mockery standing in for disclosure; a peer and family culture that codes care as weakness.
Uncertainty
What he is actually experiencing — depression, a physical health condition, crushing work conditions, or all three. Distress may appear as withdrawal, joking, anger, risk-taking, or somatic symptoms; no one presentation should be required before suffering is believed.
Affected people
Him, centrally; the cousin who noticed; everyone who would carry the loss if the decline continues unaddressed.
Power
The stigma system holds leverage only as long as his legitimacy depends on it. Research consistently associates stronger endorsement of restrictive norms — compulsory self-reliance, emotional restriction, stigma fear — with less favorable attitudes toward psychological support. [R10] [R11]
Capacity
Sleep loss erodes the very functions needed to organize help-seeking — planning, energy, hope. That is a reason to lower the threshold for reaching him, not to wait for him to perform readiness.
Alternatives
The cousin can ask directly — including, if warning signs are present, asking plainly: "Are you thinking about suicide?" Direct inquiry does not increase suicidal thoughts or behavior; the evidence-based steps are ask, be there, help keep safe, connect, and follow up. [R9] If therapy is too coded to be a first step, a primary-care appointment for the sleep is a legitimate door into care.
Individual conduct
There is no misconduct here — only a man being moved away from care by a standard someone else defined. Seeking help is not a conduct failure. His self-mockery is a failure code turned inward: a limited fact ("I am struggling") converted into a total judgment ("I am not built for this").
Environmental conditions
The peer culture and the family script are the mechanism. Locate the source, name the standard, identify the leverage — and the code loses jurisdiction.
Protection
Male life is not negotiable. A man does not have to become productive, admired, or useful before his continued existence is treated as worth protecting. Protecting male life is the framework's permanent free-access commitment.
Consequence
None is warranted against anyone. The consequence frame is itself the error to avoid: this is a health event, not a moral one.
Repair
Rebuilding more than one point of human support, so that no single job, group, or person is the only structure holding the life together.
Prevention
Follow-up beyond the acute moment — and a peer culture, changed one honest conversation at a time, in which asking for care costs nothing masculine at all.
If this scenario is your situation

If you are thinking about suicide, or you are worried about someone who might be: in the United States, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline — call, text, or chat, with Deaf and hard-of-hearing access). In immediate danger, call 911. Free, 24/7, no account, no gate. More care resources →

Principles applied: protecting male life; ask directly · failure codes · no single male psychology.

5 · Severe sleep deprivation: mitigation without immunity

A hospital porter working rotating double shifts through a staffing shortage — and awake at night with a newborn — snaps harshly at a patient's daughter, misroutes a specimen delivery, and, when first questioned, denies the mistake. Later in the shift he admits it.

Known facts
Severe accumulated sleep debt; harsh words to a bystander; a misrouted specimen with a delayed result; an initial denial followed by an admission.
Uncertainty
How much impairment contributed to each act; whether the workload pattern is documented; whether the specimen delay changed the patient's care.
Affected people
The patient whose result was delayed; the daughter who absorbed the harshness; the porter; the colleagues working the same rota.
Power
The employer sets the rota and the staffing level; the porter controls his own words and his own honesty.
Capacity
Sleep deprivation can genuinely degrade inhibition, attention, and working memory — functions of living systems, directly relevant to the snap and the misroute. [R1] It is far less relevant to the denial, which was a choice made to protect an image. Exhaustion does not choose dishonesty.
Alternatives
His: flag exhaustion before the shift, request a swap, slow down on safety-critical steps. The employer's were larger: staffing, rota design, fatigue-reporting routes that carry no stigma.
Individual conduct
Separate the three acts. The snap: mitigated by state, still owed an apology. The misroute: substantially a fatigue-and-system event. The denial: his, fully. Biological influence may modify the form of responsibility; it must be neither ignored nor allowed to erase it. Mitigation is not immunity.
Environmental conditions
An employer that degrades sleep and recovery and then reads deteriorating performance as an inexplicable character defect has performed an incomplete analysis. The rota is part of the evidence.
Protection
Patient safety first: trace and correct the specimen chain before any question of blame.
Consequence
Proportionate and separated: apology for the snap; procedural review for the misroute; the honesty lapse addressed as conduct. Discipline that ignores the rota mislocates the cause; an excuse that ignores the denial mislocates the character question.
Repair
Apology to the daughter; correction of the record; the employer's acknowledgment of the staffing conditions to the team that has been carrying them.
Prevention
Rota reform, protected recovery time, and a fatigue-reporting channel that is answered rather than punished — verified in practice, not announced.

Principles applied: operational morality; health-supported responsibility; institutional responsibility · the proportionality standard.

6 · Fatherhood and provision: without compulsory self-erasure

After his partner's hours are cut, a father of three takes every overtime block available. Eighteen months later he has an untreated hypertension diagnosis, no remaining friendships, and a habit of missing his children's events in order to fund them. He calls it "what a father does" and refuses to discuss reducing his hours.

Known facts
Sustained overwork; a diagnosed condition without follow-up; the role has consumed sleep, health, and connection; the provision itself is real and valuable.
Uncertainty
How much flexibility the household budget actually has; his cardiovascular trajectory if nothing changes.
Affected people
Him; his children — who are losing their father to the funding of their childhood; his partner, who is watching it happen.
Power
The income pressure is real. But the standard being enforced — uninterrupted provision as the measure of male worth — is a social verdict, not a household necessity. Who defined the standard, and who benefits from his dissatisfaction?
Capacity
Fatigue and untreated illness are quietly shrinking the very capacities he provides with. The body does not recognize provider status as a substitute for care.
Alternatives
A budget reviewed together; the hypertension treated; one protected rest day; some overtime blocks declined. Provision that includes the provider is still provision.
Individual conduct
No one here has wronged anyone. The question is sovereignty: he is one of the people his responsibilities must include. Self-erasure is not a duty, and no one — including his own internalized standard — may demand it as proof of fatherhood.
Environmental conditions
Provider pressure is a recognized extraction channel: overwork, injury concealment, rest coded as weakness, income as male worth. A meaningful role becomes a compulsory qualification system when refusal feels like ceasing to be a legitimate man.
Protection
His cardiovascular health is itself a family-protection issue. The most provider-like act available to him is the medical appointment.
Consequence
None — this is a sovereignty-test scenario, not a misconduct scenario: is the pursuit serving a real need, or protecting him from a social verdict? Is there a point at which it becomes enough? Would he still recognize his dignity if he earned less?
Repair
Medical follow-up; a renegotiated load; the family saying out loud that his worth was never his output. Provision should expand freedom — including his.
Prevention
Household norms that treat rest as legitimate, and a definition of fatherhood his sons can inherit without it consuming them too.

Principles applied: do not abandon yourself · the sovereignty test; provider-status extraction · provision without leverage.

7 · School discipline: recognizing conduct while rejecting sex-based presumption

Two 14-year-olds fight in a corridor. One boy threw the first punch — after weeks of taunting about his stutter that had been reported twice without action. The school suspends him under a zero-tolerance policy. A note enters his file observing that "boys this age are aggressive." The taunting reports are not reviewed.

Known facts
He struck first; the injury was minor; two prior reports about sustained taunting were filed and not acted on; the policy was applied automatically; a sex-based generalization entered the record.
Uncertainty
The full sequence of the fight; the other student's account; what the boy had tried between the second report and the punch.
Affected people
Both students — the taunting must stop as surely as the hitting; bystanders; both families; the class watching what the institution rewards and punishes.
Power
The school holds the discipline process, the record, and the narrative about both children. The boys hold very little — which is precisely why institutional process quality matters most here.
Capacity
He is fourteen. Developmental stage calibrates — it does not exempt — what self-regulation could reasonably be expected, especially under weeks of accumulating humiliation.
Alternatives
His realistic alternatives had been tried: he reported, twice, and the system did not answer. The school's alternatives were wide: timely feedback, intervention on the taunting, de-escalation before the corridor.
Individual conduct
Throwing the punch was his conduct. It needs accurate naming and a proportionate response — and naming it requires no theory of boys. "Boys this age are aggressive" converts one child's act into a sex verdict, and simultaneously functions as an alibi for the school's own failure to act on the reports. The anti-scapegoating rule cuts both ways: no collective guilt, no automatic immunity.
Environmental conditions
Schools owe safety, procedural fairness, timely feedback, and meaningful alternatives. Ask: what humiliations became routine? What feedback was withheld? An unanswered reporting system is an environmental condition, not background noise.
Protection
Both students are children owed protection: the taunted boy from further humiliation, the other from further violence — neither at the other's expense.
Consequence
A proportionate consequence for the punch that recognizes the pattern it broke out of; institutional accountability for the ignored reports; removal of the generalization from the record. Zero-tolerance automation is the opposite of proportionality, which weighs severity, pattern, provocation, and developmental stage.
Repair
Between the students, only if voluntary on both sides. From the institution: a corrected file, a reviewed reporting process, and an honest account to both families.
Prevention
Reports acted on within a defined time; discipline policy that requires human judgment; staff language standards that describe conduct, not sexes.

Principles applied: no scapegoating, no immunity · proportionate response · what schools owe.

8 · Institutional risk extraction: danger assigned downward, authority held upward

A utility's storm-response policy sends line crews — in this workforce, mostly men — to restore power in deteriorating weather. Field crews cannot call a halt themselves; only a remote operations manager can, working from a dashboard with no wind data for the actual sites. The bonus structure quietly penalizes crews that request stand-downs. After repeated crew warnings about exactly this configuration, a lineman is seriously injured.

Known facts
Physical risk was assigned to the field; halt authority was held remotely; the incentive structure punished safety requests; crews had warned about the configuration; a serious injury occurred.
Uncertainty
Whether this particular weather event was foreseeable at dispatch time; what the injured man's crew radioed in the final minutes.
Affected people
The injured lineman and his family; every crew still working under the policy; customers without power; the manager operating a dashboard designed to be blind.
Power
Policy design concentrated risk downward and authority upward. The people with the best real-time information had the least decision authority — the inverse of a defensible design, and a textbook case of extraction: the institution consumed risk tolerance and called it toughness.
Capacity
No individual capacity question drives this scenario; the impaired capacity is institutional — a decision system that could not perceive what its own crews could see.
Alternatives
Crew-level stop-work authority is a standard, available control elsewhere in industry; site wind data was obtainable; the incentive structure could have been made neutral on safety calls. The realistic alternatives belonged almost entirely to the institution.
Individual conduct
If a crew member cut a corner, that remains reviewable on its own terms. Environmental analysis is not an acquittal — it is a method for locating responsibility accurately.
Environmental conditions
Ask what was rewarded (speed), what was punished (stand-down requests), who benefited (restoration metrics), and who bore the costs (the crews). Repeated warnings made the harm reasonably foreseeable to the people holding authority.
Protection
The injured worker's care; an immediate interim stop-work rule while the policy is rebuilt.
Consequence
The corrected foreseeability chain is fully satisfied at the policy level: foreseeable harm, materially preventable by proportionate means, held by actors with knowledge, authority, capacity, control, and duty, who did not act. Accountability here is institutional first, and it is not discharged by finding a field-level scapegoat.
Repair
Care and compensation; corrected demerit records for every past stand-down request; an honest account to the workforce of what the policy did.
Prevention
Authority realigned with risk; site-level weather data; incentive neutrality on safety calls — and verification that the changes operate. A harmful mechanism is not dismantled because it was condemned; access, incentives, and authority must actually change.

Principles applied: risk extraction and the authority distinction · environmental evidence · falsifiability of "we fixed it".

9 · Protection or control: after a crisis, who decides when trust returns?

Eight months after a psychiatric hospitalization that followed a suicidal crisis, a 30-year-old man is stable in treatment and back at work, living with family. His brother still manages his bank account, reads his mail, drives him to appointments he could attend alone, and has vetoed his planned move into an apartment "until we're sure." There are no criteria for "sure," no review date, and no appeal.

Known facts
A genuine crisis eight months ago; current treatment adherence and stable work; comprehensive ongoing restrictions — money, mail, movement, housing — with no restoration criteria, review date, or appeal route.
Uncertainty
Relapse risk is real and cannot be waved away. But it must be assessed with specific, current evidence, decision by decision — not presumed indefinitely from the fact of a past crisis.
Affected people
The man, whose adult life is being administered; the family, acting from real fear and real love — this scenario has no villain, which is exactly why it needs a standard.
Power
The family controls money, information, and movement. He currently controls almost nothing — including the evidence that would prove he could.
Capacity
A past crisis is not a standing diagnosis of incapacity. Capacity is decision-specific and current; support for the exercise of one's own authority — not its replacement — is the governing standard. [R6]
Alternatives
Supported decision-making that he directs: a joint safety plan with agreed relapse indicators, a supporter he chooses, a staged return of the account, agreed check-ins around the move — support that strengthens authorship instead of quietly replacing the author.
Individual conduct
His part: honesty about treatment and genuine participation in the safety plan. Sovereignty is bounded and relational — it is exercised with the people who love him, not against them.
Environmental conditions
Fear without a framework becomes surveillance. Permanent scrutiny produces compliance while weakening self-government: he begins organizing his life around avoiding suspicion rather than rebuilding judgment. Predictability produced through surveillance destroys the freedom it claims to protect.
Protection
Suicide risk deserves absolute seriousness. [R8] But protection that preserves life must also preserve the person: protection preserves autonomy; control disguised as protection consumes it. A life emptied of self-government is not the safe outcome it appears to be.
Consequence
No one has committed misconduct; the question is the legitimacy of restriction. Apply the Autonomy Restoration Standard: define the specific concern, define each exact restriction and its evidence, use the least restrictive response, set measurable restoration criteria and a review date, restore when criteria are met rather than moving the standard, and provide appeal. A restriction without a path to restoration is control, not correction.
Repair
The mail and the account come back now — no current, decision-specific evidence justifies them. What continues is what he chooses to keep as support.
Prevention
Written restoration criteria agreed while things are calm, so that love does not calcify into administration — and so the next hard moment has a plan instead of a takeover.

Principles applied: trust must produce freedom; Autonomy Restoration Standard · supported sovereignty · the role-to-oppression diagnostic.

10 · A public claim meets new evidence: how a framework corrects itself

This scenario is real, and the institution in it is this one. Early project material framed male biological integrity around "100% health" — a percentage standard implying that full health was the platform of male self-government. Later review found the claim indefensible: no universal percentage scale converts health into moral capacity, and a perfection standard silently ranks disabled and chronically ill men against an imagined ideal body. The standard was replaced.

Known facts
The earlier claim was published; critical review found it scientifically and ethically unsound; the governing standard is now each man's best attainable and sustainable biological integrity, relative to his actual condition, age, disability, access to care, and baseline.
Uncertainty
Permanent, by design. Any current claim could be revised by better evidence — and the framework must say what evidence would change each conclusion.
Affected people
Readers — above all the disabled and chronically ill men whom a perfection standard would have quietly injured while claiming to defend them.
Power
A publisher holds narrative power. A visible correction culture is how that power is kept accountable: a framework centered on scrutiny cannot exempt itself from scrutiny.
Capacity
Institutional, here: does the institution have a working mechanism for metabolizing being wrong — version history, review, a public register — or only a habit of confidence?
Alternatives
Three were available: the quiet edit (which erodes trust when discovered), defensive doubling down (which converts an error into an identity), and the public, dated correction. The third was chosen.
Individual conduct
For an institution, conduct means publishing the correction visibly, preserving the superseded wording as history, and not punishing the critics who exposed the weakness. Criticism is not hostility; serious objections must produce visible revision.
Environmental conditions
Most information environments reward confidence and punish revision — audiences read corrections as weakness, so platforms learn never to issue them. A framework that demands answerability from men must model it under the same incentives.
Protection
Readers are protected by labeled evidence: every substantive claim placed on the evidence ladder, from systematic review down to emerging hypothesis, so that synthesis is never dressed as settled science.
Consequence
The superseded claim is retired and recorded on the public corrections register — not deleted, because the record of having been wrong is part of the evidence that the method works.
Repair
The correction itself, plus claim cards that disclose sources, counterevidence, limitations, review dates, and what evidence would change the conclusion.
Prevention
Falsifiability discipline: operational claims carry observable completion criteria, and the platform never claims that reading content reduces suicide, prevents violence, or restores health without a valid evaluation design. Engagement is not dignity. Visibility is not impact.

Principles applied: evidence ladder; claim cards; corrections · the corrected biological-integrity standard.
