Paid value without junkThe standard for paid value

The subscription must not be a paywall around generic encouragement, repetitive AI prose, superficial quizzes, or an endless library. Paid value should provide depth, structured application, human guidance, access, continuity, and care navigation. Core dignity, safety information, crisis routing, accessibility, and basic provider-finding must remain free.

We do not sell manhood. We provide serious resources for protecting the health, integrity, and self-government of the man who already possesses it.

Planning targets, not offersFuture clinical-price targets

The project publishes the following planning targets for future professional care:

  • Standard fifty-minute therapy session: $65–$90.
  • Protected sliding-scale appointment: $40–$70.
  • Approximately $130–$170 for two monthly sessions.
  • Approximately $240–$320 for four monthly sessions.
  • One-session package target: about $89/month.
  • Two-session package target: about $149/month.
  • Four-session package target: $259–$319/month.

Affordable-therapy planning can benchmark nonprofit sliding-scale networks such as Open Path, which currently advertises individual sessions in the United States at $40–$70 — while recognizing that availability, specialty, location, licensure, and insurance vary.[R28] Target ranges are published as planning goals, never as universal promises.

These are not current offers

Published price targets create reputational and consumer-protection obligations even when labeled as plans. Before any price remains public at launch, the project must verify provider compensation, licensing overhead, insurance administration, no-show rates, taxes, platform costs, clinical documentation, supervision, accessibility, crisis coverage, refund boundaries, and geographic availability.

Corporate accountabilityThe quality guarantee

Published policy — this is the standard for future paid enrollment, not a present transaction right, because no payment infrastructure is live.

Every paid tier will include a visible 100% Money-Back Quality Guarantee tied to published standards. The company guarantees honest representation, functioning access, professional credentials, promised depth, accessibility, privacy, moderation, support, and delivery — not a particular clinical, emotional, relationship, or life outcome.

The published future guarantee promises a full refund for eligible membership payments during the first thirty days, and a fairly calculated prorated response when promised service remains materially unavailable later. Refund requests will be available through account settings without a retention call, intimate disclosure, proof of completing every program, or a hidden processing deduction. The guarantee rejects intimate disclosure as a condition of refund and treats complaints as evidence. Completed professional sessions may follow a separate policy, while missed, inaccessible, legally unavailable, technically failed, or materially misrepresented sessions will be refunded or replaced.

Published quality standards

  • Original, professionally edited editorial content.
  • Practical tools with defined purposes and outcomes.
  • Accurate plan descriptions and functioning promised features.
  • Responsive support and clear billing.
  • Appropriately moderated communities.
  • Transparent credentials and role boundaries.
  • Accessible mobile and desktop experiences.
  • No hidden AI substitution for human care.
  • No crisis upselling, masculinity ranking, targeted advertising from health data, or dependency-based retention.
  • No material difference between advertising and delivery.

What the guarantee still requires operationally

The guarantee is ethically strong. Operationally, before payment ever opens, it requires:

  • an exact eligibility definition;
  • automated and human refund paths;
  • response-time commitments;
  • records of what was promised at purchase;
  • handling of annual plans, upgrades, credits, and partial use;
  • separate rules for delivered professional time;
  • fraud review with appeal;
  • and consistent application across web and mobile.
The reconciliation requirement

Any headline “100% guarantee” must be reconciled with the separate concept of prorating annual-plan relief after thirty days. A full-refund promise and a prorated-remedy promise can both be legitimate in different periods, but they are not the same guarantee. Before payment opens, the project will publish one unambiguous hierarchy covering the initial refund window, later material unavailability, delivered professional time, annual plans, taxes, credits, and jurisdictional rights.

Until payment infrastructure exists, the “quality guarantee” remains explicitly what it is on this page: the standard for future paid enrollment, not a present transaction right.

Our standards apply to us, too. If we do not deliver what we promised, we acknowledge it, return the eligible payment, and examine what must change.

Honest labelsProfessional roles are never blurred

Peer support, coaching, education, and clinical care must never be blurred. SAMHSA describes peer support as person-centered, voluntary, relationship-focused, trauma-informed, and grounded in lived experience; it extends support but does not replace licensed treatment.[R27] Telebehavioral health professionals must be legally authorized where the patient is located, and providers should verify patient location and emergency planning before appointments.[R24] The full role-boundary architecture — peer support, coaching, navigation, psychotherapy, psychiatry, and crisis response as distinct roles — is published on the Find Care page.

Published self-criticismThe business-model critique

This critique is part of the record because commercial honesty is part of the framework. The proposed prices are accessible but may be operationally optimistic, especially at the professional tier. A $49 monthly fee that includes a private care consultation, a private peer-support session, navigation, matching, and reduced therapy pricing can become financially unsustainable unless utilization, staffing, scheduling, supervision, and jurisdictional scope are modeled conservatively.

The project must not subsidize low prices by:

  • underpaying clinicians, peers, moderators, or navigators;
  • using unlicensed labor for clinical functions;
  • replacing human review with opaque AI;
  • overselling access that is not available;
  • restricting refunds;
  • extracting health data for advertising;
  • or building engagement dependency.
The safest rollout order

Open Access first, then Integrity Membership, then a small jurisdiction-limited Guided Practice pilot, and only later a professionally governed care-access layer — each step through its launch gate before enrollment opens.

Non-negotiablesCommercial-ethics non-negotiables

No paid tier opens until every item below holds. These sit inside the wider pre-launch audit and the master completeness standard.

  • Core dignity content, the glossary, safety information, accessibility, crisis pathways, official care navigation, complaints, and cancellation remain free.
  • Paid features demonstrate specific usable value.
  • No streaks, fake urgency, shame-based retention, crisis upselling, or masculinity ranking.
  • Transparent pricing and professional fees.
  • Easy online cancellation and pause.
  • 100% quality guarantee tied to published standards.
  • No emotional vulnerability used for targeted sales.
  • Refund data used for service improvement.

The compact test behind all of it: every paid feature must answer what the user can understand, decide, communicate, change, repair, document, or access because it exists. Crisis, cancellation, refund, privacy, and access assistance work without premium status — always.
