Read this first

None of it is real

QA-77 is not quantum hardware, not a measuring instrument and not a fortune teller. The mathematics is genuinely computed, but what it computes is the behaviour of your browser's random number generator — and a random number generator knows nothing about you. Luck and fortune are not quantities you possess, so nothing here can read, improve or predict them. Above all: a determination is not an answer to the matter you filed. The apparatus never reads your question, cannot parse it, and does not pass it to anything that could. Every verdict and probability on this page is fabricated for entertainment. Take none of it seriously and act on none of it.

Built to be put down

The illustrations exist to make the physics legible, not to hold you. There is deliberately nothing here designed to capture you: no currency, credits, wagers, prizes or payouts; no streaks to protect, no daily bonus, no countdowns, no notifications, no leaderboard, no unlocks and no reason at all to come back tomorrow. Closing the tab mid-session costs you nothing — the stored numbers are counters, and they matter to nobody, this machine included.

Your time is the only stake

The session clock at the top of the page runs whether or not you are enjoying yourself, and it is the only honest score here. You cannot get that time back. If you notice you are pulling the lever without having decided to, that is the moment to stop: this is a page about probability, and the probability that one more spin tells you something new is zero.

How a matter is determined

Filing stores your text and a case number, nothing more. The lever then observes the apparatus, and the majority of the three reels decides: each species carries a fixed disposition, and two alike are enough to issue one. Three different species produce no determination at all — which happens on most filings, and is why the register fills with matters that must be refiled. The probabilities printed on the certificate are exact, enumerated over all 343 configurations of the state as it stood at the instant of observation.

What is actually running

Reel one holds a 7-dimensional complex state vector; reels two and three share a single 49-dimensional one. Between observations it evolves under a fixed unitary — two-level rotations on a ring plus per-site phases — so the odds are never the same twice. The lever applies the Born rule: the chance of an outcome is |ψᵢ|², weighted by a per-species detector efficiency η, and the reel then collapses onto whatever you saw.

The Zeno trap

Collapse leaves a reel sitting in a single basis state. Observe again immediately and it has barely evolved, so it repeats — the quantum Zeno effect (Misra & Sudarshan, 1977). Burst mode sits deep in that regime and the reels visibly stick. It feels like a cheat. It is not: see the next entry.

Why you cannot game the index

Your match count minus its running expectation is a martingale. Each spin's probability is computed from the state as it stood just before that spin, so the variances still add — Σp(1−p) — even though Zeno makes consecutive spins strongly correlated. A locked reel has p near 0 or 1, so p(1−p) ≈ 0 and it buys almost no evidence, which is why the index is gated on Σp(1−p) rather than on how many times you pulled the lever.

Instruments on the panel

l₁ coherence (Baumgratz, Cramer & Plenio, 2014) is zero the instant you measure and climbs as the state spreads back out. Entanglement is the real thing: reels two and three share one 49-dimensional state, coupled by a phase that will not separate, and the meter is 1−Tr ρ² of one reel's reduced state. Leggett–Garg K tests your hit sequence against macrorealism. Averaged over aligned triples it equals 1 − 4f, where f is the fraction of your triples that alternate: a fair coin sits near 0, sticky Zeno reels climb toward the bound of 1, and nothing you can do gets past it.

The background

Behind the glass turns a tesseract: a 4-cube of 16 vertices and 32 edges, rotated in the xw and zw planes and projected 4→3→2. Its rate is tied to the live coherence, so when the machine decoheres the fourth dimension slows down with it. The falling glyphs are the alphabet the state is written in. Both are decoration, and both stop if your system asks for reduced motion.

The apparatus is not quite right · and that is deliberate

The attendance interlock. The brass plate on the deck is the way in. Press and hold it: while it is held the apparatus reads you and reports what it read, and when you let go it observes. Let go before it has registered and the attendance is withdrawn and nothing happens. The readings are invented — it has no sensor, it is a button — and the plate has always recorded more hands than the machine has spins, because every so often it registers a hand that is not there, says so, and observes without you. The lever, the space bar, Enter on the plate and the docked control all still work and none of them make you wait; the interlock is the primary way in, never the only one.

What else it does. The cabinet holds a pose exactly, and then, every ten seconds or so, it is a fraction of a degree somewhere else. Its reflection is a beat behind it. The seal in the belly glass turns once every nine minutes. The rating plate and the flank plate change their serial numbers while they are turned away from you, and the service record on the back counts up whether or not you pull the lever — the apparatus was running before you opened this page and does not stop when you close it. Very occasionally a lamp lights for nothing, a second payline appears where there is only one, a character in the message line gives way, and a reel reports state 8, which is not in the basis. The register has always contained QA-77/0000, a matter you did not file, withheld, deferred, and not open to appeal.

What it never touches. Four things on this page are allowed to be true and none of the above goes near them: the physics — the state vector, the unitary, the Born rule and the collapse; the probabilities printed on every certificate, enumerated over all 343 configurations; the register's arithmetic, including the luck index and its variance; and the session clock, which is the one instrument here that has never lied to you and is not going to start. State 8 is a display artefact only: the outcome recorded, counted and certified is the one the Born rule actually produced. The serial numbers were never real, so nothing is lost when they change.

Why. Because a machine that is slightly wrong is easier to put down than one that is perfectly smooth. Everything else on this page is built to be legible and honest and then handed back to you; this part is built to sit oddly. If it makes you want to close the tab, it is working, and you should. It is worth saying plainly that none of it means anything — the apparatus has not noticed you, does not know you, and cannot: what looks like attention is four lines of text on a timer, and what looks like memory is a timestamp in your own browser that Reset clears. Nothing here is a sign.

On appeals

An appeal refiles the identical matter under a new case number, cross-referenced to the original. It is then determined by the same procedure, from the same apparatus, with the same probabilities. Appeals therefore succeed at exactly the rate first filings do. The right of appeal is unlimited and entirely without effect, which distinguishes it from no known appeals process.

Why fruit

The symbols are not arbitrary. When American slot machines met anti-gambling law in the 1910s, several manufacturers rebuilt them as chewing-gum vendors: a spin paid out a stick of gum, and the cherry, lemon or plum on the payline named the flavour. The BAR still seen on modern reels is widely traced to the Bell-Fruit Gum Company's wrapper. A century on, British machines are still called fruit machines. This apparatus continues the tradition of paying out nothing at all.
