Why fruit?

The symbols are not decoration. When American slot machines ran into anti-gambling law in the 1910s, several manufacturers rebuilt them as gum vendors: a spin paid out a stick of chewing gum, and the cherry, lemon or plum on the payline named the flavour you had won.

The BAR that still turns up on modern reels is widely traced to the wrapper of the Bell-Fruit Gum Company. A century on, British machines are still called fruit machines — the produce outlived the gum.

What the index means

Hits follow a binomial distribution, so your count can be scored against it. The index is (hits − expected) ÷ standard deviation: zero is exactly average luck, +2 is the top few percent, −2 the bottom few.

It drifts toward zero the longer you play. That is the house's only real edge here, and it costs you nothing.
